# Local SEO / Maps / Entity Visibility Reference (2026)

**Reviewed:** 2026-08-26  
**Primary first-party source:** [Google Business Profile — Tips to improve local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en)

## 1. Google's disclosed local ranking model

Google says local results are mainly based on:

1. **Relevance** — how well a Business Profile matches what the user is searching for.
2. **Distance** — how far the business is from the searcher/location context.
3. **Prominence** — how well-known the business is; Google cites signals such as links and reviews among inputs.

Google explicitly says there is no way to request or pay Google for a better local organic ranking.

Do not turn these three categories into fake percentages; Google does not disclose fixed weights.

## 2. Business Profile foundation

For a legitimate eligible business:

- verify ownership/representation;
- use the real business name according to platform rules;
- keep category selection accurate;
- maintain complete address/service-area details as applicable;
- keep phone, website, hours and special hours current;
- maintain products/services where supported;
- use real representative photos/videos;
- respond to reviews professionally;
- keep duplicate profiles controlled.

Completeness helps Google understand relevance but no individual field guarantees rank.

## 3. NAP/entity consistency

Maintain consistent core identity across authoritative properties:

- official site;
- Google Business Profile;
- Bing Places / Apple/maps ecosystem where relevant;
- major industry/local directories;
- social/company profiles;
- structured data;
- legal/contact/about pages.

Minor formatting differences are not the real problem; contradictory identity facts are. Prioritize accurate name/location/phone/URL/entity relationships.

## 4. Location pages

A useful location page should genuinely help a user in that location, for example with:

- services/products actually available there;
- real address/service area;
- hours/contact methods;
- local team/context;
- directions/parking/access information when useful;
- localized pricing/availability/policies when different;
- unique testimonials/case studies where genuine;
- local photos;
- local FAQs driven by real user needs.

Avoid mass city pages that only swap the city name while offering no distinct value. Such pages can become doorway/scaled-content problems.

## 5. Reviews

Google’s local ranking help says review quantity and positive ratings can contribute to prominence/local ranking.

Sustainable review practice:

- request reviews from real customers neutrally;
- make the review process easy but not coercive;
- do not buy/fabricate reviews;
- do not selectively gate unhappy users away from review platforms in ways that violate platform rules;
- respond to feedback;
- analyze recurring complaints operationally.

Treat reviews as both reputation data and customer research, not just an SEO score.

## 6. Local links/citations

Useful local authority sources may include:

- chambers/industry associations;
- genuine sponsorships/partnerships;
- local press/news coverage;
- municipal/education/community resources where relevant;
- suppliers/distributors;
- niche directories used by real customers;
- local events/resources your business genuinely participates in.

Do not mass-submit to thousands of low-quality directories merely to inflate citation counts.

## 7. On-site local signals

Important pages should clearly communicate:

- what the business does;
- where it operates;
- who it serves;
- how to contact/visit;
- real evidence of local operations.

Use city/region terminology naturally where it helps users. Avoid stuffing every nearby city into titles/headings/footer blocks.

## 8. Local structured data

Use appropriate `Organization`/`LocalBusiness` subtypes only for real facts. Keep structured data consistent with visible page/profile data.

Schema does not replace Business Profile verification and is not a guaranteed map-pack ranking boost.

## 9. Multi-location architecture

For real locations:

- one stable canonical URL per location;
- parent “Locations” discovery page;
- crawlable links to locations;
- distinct local facts/content;
- location-specific schema where accurate;
- correct profile→location-page mapping;
- closed/moved location lifecycle process.

Do not create profile/location combinations for virtual offices or fake addresses in violation of platform policies.

## 10. Service-area businesses

Follow Business Profile eligibility/address-display rules. On-site service-area pages should correspond to real operating coverage and provide useful local context rather than hundreds of near-duplicate pages.

## 11. Local search measurement

Track separately:

- Business Profile impressions/actions;
- calls/directions/website visits where platform data supports them;
- organic local landing-page clicks/conversions;
- local pack/map rankings sampled across geography;
- branded vs non-branded queries;
- review volume/rating/response time;
- local citation/referral traffic;
- conversion quality/revenue.

A single rank checked from one office computer is not representative of a geographically variable local SERP.

## 12. Grid ranking caution

Geo-grid tools can visualize local visibility but results depend on:

- coordinates;
- query;
- time;
- device/context;
- personalization/platform behavior.

Use them as repeated observations, not universal truth.

## 13. Local AI/discovery layer

AI/search assistants can use combinations of:

- web pages;
- business/map/profile data;
- reviews/reputation;
- third-party directories;
- search-index sources.

The safe strategy is factual entity consistency + accessible first-party content + genuine reputation, not fabricated cross-web mentions.

## 14. Local SEO checklist

- [ ] Business is eligible and verified.
- [ ] Profile fields are accurate/complete.
- [ ] Primary/secondary categories are truthful.
- [ ] Hours/special hours current.
- [ ] Website landing URL is correct/canonical.
- [ ] Real photos/products/services maintained where relevant.
- [ ] Reviews are requested ethically and answered.
- [ ] Site clearly describes services + locations.
- [ ] Real locations have useful unique pages.
- [ ] NAP/entity facts are consistent across authoritative sources.
- [ ] LocalBusiness/Organization schema is accurate.
- [ ] Local links/PR/associations are earned legitimately.
- [ ] Fake locations/doorway city pages are absent.
- [ ] Performance is measured geographically and by conversion.
