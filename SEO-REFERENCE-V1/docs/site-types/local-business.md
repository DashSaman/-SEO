# Local Business SEO Playbook

## Search surfaces

Local businesses compete in both localized organic results and Google Business Profile/local-pack surfaces. Google’s documented local framing is **relevance, distance and prominence**; distance cannot be eliminated by on-page tricks.

## Foundation

Maintain:
- accurate business name;
- real address/service area;
- phone/site/hours;
- correct primary/secondary categories;
- services/products;
- current photos;
- legitimate attributes;
- GBP verification/ownership.

Never keyword-stuff the business name or create fake locations.

## Website architecture

`Home → Services → Location(s) → Supporting guides/case studies/contact`

Create a location page only where there is a real location/service-area distinction and useful local evidence.

## Location page evidence

Include when applicable:
- address/service area;
- hours/contact;
- directions/map;
- staff/team;
- local availability/pricing/logistics;
- location-specific case studies/reviews;
- neighborhood/coverage detail useful to customers;
- LocalBusiness schema matching visible truth.

Avoid city-name swap templates.

## Reviews

Ask real customers consistently and ethically. No review gating, buying or fake accounts. Respond to recurring operational complaints and keep policy-compliant review links/processes.

## Citations

Prioritize authoritative customer-used directories, industry associations and local platforms. Keep NAP accurate. Do not buy thousands of low-quality directory submissions.

## Local authority

Earn links/mentions through real community/industry relationships, local data/resources, sponsorships with proper link qualification, events, chambers/associations and customer/partner evidence.

## Query mapping

Map distinct services to service pages; map genuine location intent to location pages. Do not create separate pages for every near-synonym if intent is the same.

## Measurement

Track:
- GBP/local performance metrics;
- local pack rankings with grid/context where available;
- localized organic rankings;
- calls/directions/bookings/forms;
- review volume/sentiment;
- branded/local search demand;
- per-location revenue/leads.

## PASS gate

PASS when business facts are accurate, locations are real, service/location pages contain unique proof, reviews are authentic, NAP is consistent on important platforms and measurement uses actual local contexts instead of one global rank.
